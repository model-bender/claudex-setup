# Schedule a Routine for the Moment You Will Act on Its Output, Not When the Data Is Ready

## TL;DR

Pick a routine's run time by asking when you will actually read and act on the result. Identical output delivered at the wrong hour gets skimmed and lost.

## Why it matters

Austin Marchese calls the obvious answer a trap: a daily brief 'seems obvious, right, run it in the morning, well, not if your mornings are crazy busy because that overview email will just get lost and you won't actually get value from it.' He runs his own at night because he prepares for the next day the evening before. His framing is that most automations fail on impact rather than on output: 'a daily brief automation can have an identical output, but depending on when it's sent has a dramatically different impact.' The failure is silent, the routine keeps succeeding while you stop reading it.

## How to apply

For each routine, name the moment you will act on its output and schedule for shortly before that, not for when the source data finishes updating. Revisit the time when your routine changes rather than assuming the schedule still fits. If you cannot name the moment you will act, that is a signal the automation should not exist yet.
