# Brief /design Like a Client: Who It Is For, the One Job of the Page, and How Many Sections

## TL;DR

A /design brief that names the audience, the single action the page must produce, and an explicit section count returns structured concepts instead of guesses. Drop any of the three and Claude fills the gap itself.

## Why it matters

Tristen O'Brien's worked example is 'I want a landing page for my local dog grooming business, the only job of the page is to get people to book, and I want five different sections'. His summary of the whole command is 'talk to it like you're talking to a client, tell it who it's for, tell it what the page has to do', and he attributes structured rather than guessed output to exactly that. Without the job statement the generator optimises for looking finished rather than for the conversion you actually want, and without a section count you get whatever length it feels like.

## How to apply

Write the /design brief as three declarative clauses before any style talk: audience ('my local dog grooming business'), the single job ('the only job of this page is to get people to book'), and structure ('five sections'). Style references come after, not instead of, those three. Keep the job singular: two jobs in one brief is what produces the unfocused page.
