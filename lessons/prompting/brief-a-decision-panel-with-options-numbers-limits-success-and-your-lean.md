# Brief a Decision Panel With the Decision, 2-3 Options, Real Numbers, Limits, a Definition of Success and Your Current Lean

## TL;DR

Herk's checklist for a council question: the decision itself, at least two or three options, real figures, hard limits, what success looks like, and which way you currently lean and why.

## Why it matters

Herk says options matter because without them it is hard to categorize the paths, and seven advisors may each invent a different option and overwhelm you, when really all seven are saying some version of option B. Numbers such as revenue and team size contextualize the choice, limits (deadlines, hours, budgets) bound it, and the success definition says why you need the decision at all. He includes your current lean because AI tends to agree with you, but says the panel is set up not to just validate it. He does not blindly take the verdict and reads each perspective because it changes how he thinks.

## How to apply

Write the question with: 1) the decision to make; 2) two or three options; 3) real numbers; 4) deadlines, hours and budgets; 5) what good looks like and why you need the decision; 6) your lean and reasoning. If Claude Code is missing context it will ask or pull it from your connected workspace. You can also talk through it and ask Claude to turn that into a council prompt.
Tension: another lesson advises not leaking your conclusion. Herk includes the lean because the panel's personas have their own principles, which is his claim and was not tested here.
Checked: no flag or setting is named.
