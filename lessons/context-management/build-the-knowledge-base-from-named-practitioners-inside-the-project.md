# Build the Knowledge Base From Named Practitioners' Own Material, and Keep It In the Project Rather Than a Separate Notebook App

## TL;DR

When you need the lived-experience answer rather than the textbook one, pick specific practitioners who have solved the problem, gather their own writing and talks into files in the Claude Code project, and ask questions against those files.

## Why it matters

Austin Marchese contrasts the textbook answer (what the model averages out of training data) with the mentor answer, and his sourcing rule is to bias toward experts with a public online presence precisely because there is reference material to load. He notes the simple route is a separate notebook tool, but he keeps the corpus in the Claude project instead, which is what lets the session that answers the question also act on the answer in your code.

## How to apply

Ask Claude to shortlist practitioners who have publicly solved your specific problem, then collect their posts, transcripts and talks (a crawler or fetch tool helps) into a folder in the project. Tell Claude to answer from those files only, and to say when the corpus does not cover the question, so you can see when you are back on the training-data average. Keep it scoped to one topic per folder rather than one giant reference dump.
