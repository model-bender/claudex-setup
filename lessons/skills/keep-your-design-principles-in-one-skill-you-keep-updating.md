# Put Your Design Principles in One Skill You Keep Updating, Not in Each New Prompt

## TL;DR

Layering, scroll behaviour, motion and type principles belong in a single design skill the build invokes, so every site inherits them. Prompts then carry only what is specific to this site, and what you learn from each build goes back into the skill.

## Why it matters

Nate Herk's every good result in this video runs through his own scroll skill, and his framing is that a site built without a skill or template is probably going to feel like AI slop because the model falls back to its average. The skill is also what made two very different one-shot builds (a herb brand and a keyboard brand) both come out with correct layering from a single prompt. Re-typing the principles per prompt means the quality of a build depends on what you remembered to type that day, and improvements never accumulate.

## How to apply

Create one skill holding your reusable design rules: layering and depth, scroll behaviour, text entry, motion restraint, mobile requirements, and the verification loop. Keep per-site inputs in the prompt instead: brand guidelines, the audience and promise, and the reference sites. After each build, fold what you learned back into the skill rather than into your notes. Anthropic's Frontend Design skill is a reasonable starting point if you would rather not start blank.
